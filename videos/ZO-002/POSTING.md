## Script (as narrated)
That pop when you crack your knuckles? It isn't your bones. Every knuckle is a joint: two bones, sealed in a capsule full of slippery fluid. That fluid has gas dissolved in it, like a fizzy drink. When you pull the finger, the capsule stretches, and the pressure inside suddenly drops. The gas rushes out, and a bubble snaps into existence. That's the pop. It takes about twenty minutes for the gas to dissolve again, so you can't crack the same knuckle twice in a row. And no, it doesn't cause arthritis. One doctor cracked only his left hand for over fifty years to check. Both hands were fine.

## Fact check
- The sound coincides with a gas cavity forming in the joint fluid, shown by real-time MRI (Kawchuk et al., 2015). A 2018 modelling study argued partial bubble collapse also plays a part; the video's wording covers the MRI finding.
- A joint needs roughly 20 minutes before it can be cracked again.
- Dr Donald Unger cracked the knuckles of his left hand only, for more than 50 years, and found no arthritis in either hand (1998; Ig Nobel Prize 2009). Larger studies have also found no link to arthritis.
