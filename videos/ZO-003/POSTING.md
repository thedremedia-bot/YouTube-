## Script (as narrated)
Eat ice cream too fast, and this happens. The cold hits the roof of your mouth. Blood vessels there tighten, then suddenly widen, sending a rush of warm blood toward your brain. A nearby nerve feels that sudden change, and fires off a pain signal. But that same nerve also covers your forehead. So your brain gets confused, and the pain shows up there instead. To stop it, press your tongue against the roof of your mouth. The warmth calms the nerve, and the pain fades.

## Fact check
- Brain freeze (cold-stimulus headache) starts when something cold touches the palate; the pain is referred to the forehead through the trigeminal nerve, which serves both areas (Hulihan, BMJ 1997).
- Rapid narrowing then widening of blood vessels, with increased blood flow in the anterior cerebral artery, was measured during brain freeze (Serrador et al., 2012).
- This is the leading explanation, not a fully settled one. The video presents it plainly; if a commenter raises that, agree and pin the sources.
- Pressing the tongue to the palate to warm it is the standard advice; it is a home remedy, not a clinical trial result.
