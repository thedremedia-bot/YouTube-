# 4. Growth, monetization and analytics

## 4.1 Growth

Short-form feeds rank on how much of a video people watch and whether they
watch again, then on shares and follows. Each tactic below is tied to one of
those.

| Goal | What we do |
|---|---|
| Stop the scroll | The hook is a claim that sounds wrong ("A house cat is faster than Usain Bolt") or an open question, on screen and spoken inside 1.5 seconds, with motion from frame one |
| Watch time and retention | One idea per video; the answer is held back until the final third; a visual change every 2–3 seconds; no intro, no filler; target 85%+ average viewed on videos under 30 s |
| Rewatches | The last line hands back to the first so the loop is seamless; fast reveals that reward a second look; a second, bigger number dropped in the last two seconds |
| Shares | Facts that make the sender look clever, phrased so they can be repeated in one sentence |
| Comments | A specific question with an easy answer ("Which one surprised you: 2 or 3?"); *Fact or Cap* invites people to vote before the reveal; reply to the first 20 comments on every video |
| Followers | Named series with numbered episodes, so there is a reason to come back; a spoken follow prompt in at most one video in three |
| Returning viewers | Fixed series days (see calendar); viewer-suggested topics credited on screen ("Asked by @name"); weekly "you asked" episode from week 4 |

**Recurring series**

1. **Scale Check**: how big, far or many something really is.
2. **Fact or Cap**: a common belief, a pause to guess, the verdict.
3. **Time Warp**: timelines that feel wrong but are right.
4. **What If**: the world shrunk to a model, or a thought experiment.
5. **The Numbers Game**: sport, measured.
6. **Wild Specs**: one animal, presented like a spec sheet.

**Learning from others without copying.** Look at the top channels for size
comparison and facts content for structure only: how fast the hook lands, where
the reveal sits, how long the video runs. We never reuse their scripts, footage,
music or topics in the same order. Our facts come from primary sources.

**What we will not do:** rage-bait, fake cliffhangers, misleading titles, or
"part 2" traps. They raise views briefly and cost trust and monetization.

## 4.2 Monetization

Views, revenue and profit are three different numbers. A video with a million
views may bring in R700–R2,500 in Shorts ad revenue once monetized, and nothing
before that. Profit is revenue minus costs; costs here are close to zero, so
almost all revenue is profit, but your time is the real cost.

### Income streams, in the order they become realistic

| Stream | Available in South Africa | When | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Affiliate links | Yes | From day 1, meaningful from about 5,000 followers | Amazon Associates pays internationally; Takealot has a local programme; book and science-kit links fit the content. Link in bio and pinned comments. Disclose affiliate links. |
| Digital products | Yes | From about 10,000 followers | Printable "scale of the universe" posters, a R99–R199 fact e-book, classroom slide packs. Sell through Gumroad or Payhip, paid out via PayPal or bank. |
| Brand sponsorships | Yes | From about 20,000–50,000 followers on any one platform | Education apps, VPNs, science kits. Short-form rates vary widely; a rough guide is $5–$25 per 1,000 average views. |
| YouTube Shorts ad revenue | Yes | After YPP acceptance | Small per view; scales only with tens of millions of monthly views. |
| YouTube long-form ad revenue | Yes | After YPP acceptance | Typically many times the Shorts rate per view. Weekly 8–10 minute compilations start in month 2 and also build the watch-hours route into YPP. |
| YouTube fan funding (Super Thanks, memberships) | Yes | Lower YPP tier | Minor for this type of channel. |
| TikTok Creator Rewards | **No** | — | Not offered in South Africa. |
| TikTok brand deals, LIVE gifts | Yes | 1,000+ followers for LIVE | LIVE does not suit a faceless channel well. |
| Instagram Gifts and Subscriptions | Unclear | 500 / 10,000 followers | Sources conflict; check the dashboard. |
| Licensing videos to publishers and schools | Yes | Opportunistic | Our visuals are fully owned, so they can be licensed. |
| Second channel on the same pipeline | Yes | After month 3 | For example the football-numbers spin-off. |

**Getting paid.** YouTube pays through AdSense by bank transfer to a South
African account once the balance passes $100. It is income for SARS purposes;
keep records from the first rand. Affiliate and product platforms pay by PayPal
or bank transfer.

### Milestones

These are targets to steer by. Many channels take longer and some never get
there. Rand figures assume roughly R17–R18 to the dollar; check the current rate.

| Milestone | Target window | What has to be true |
|---|---|---|
| First 1,000 followers (any platform) | Weeks 3–8 | Daily posting; at least one video past 100,000 views |
| First 1,000 YouTube subscribers | Months 1–3 | As above, on YouTube |
| First 10,000 | Months 3–6 | One or two series reliably above average; several videos past 500,000 views |
| First income of any kind | Months 2–4 | Affiliate links live; probably tens of rands at first |
| First R1,000 earned (cumulative) | Months 3–6 | Affiliate sales plus first product or small brand deal |
| YouTube Partner Program acceptance | Months 6–12 | 1,000 subscribers plus the Shorts-views or watch-hours bar in force at the time. If the reported February 2027 increase is confirmed, this is harder and the long-form route matters more. |
| First R10,000 earned (cumulative) | Months 6–12 | YPP active or two to three sponsor deals |
| First 100,000 | Months 9–18 | A breakout format; a minority of channels reach this |
| R50,000 in a month | Months 12–24 or later | Roughly: 15–30 million monthly Shorts views, plus long-form, plus two or more sponsors a month, plus product sales. No single stream gets there alone. Not typical. |

## 4.3 Weekly review

Every 7 days I will ask you for the following. Screenshots are fine.

**YouTube Studio → Analytics**
- Overview for the last 7 days: views, watch time, subscribers gained.
- Content → Shorts: the list sorted by views, showing "viewed vs swiped away" and average percentage viewed.
- For the best two and worst two videos: the retention graph.
- Audience: top countries, and returning vs new viewers.

**TikTok → Creator tools → Analytics**
- Overview (7 days), and per video: views, average watch time, watched full video %, new followers.

**Instagram → Professional dashboard**
- Reels: plays, reach, average watch time, shares, saves, follows.

**What I do with it**

1. Rank every video by "viewed vs swiped away" (the hook) and by average percentage viewed (the body).
2. Compare by series, hook type, length and posting slot, not just by video.
3. Mark each series **scale up**, **keep testing** or **pause**. A series is paused after six videos if it stays in the bottom third on both measures.
4. Rewrite the next week's calendar: more episodes of the top two series, new hook styles borrowed from the best openers, the weakest series replaced with a new test format.
5. Report back in plain language: what worked, what did not, what changes.

Working benchmarks, to be adjusted to your own data: viewed-vs-swiped above 70%,
average percentage viewed above 85% for videos under 30 seconds, and at least
one share per 200 views.
