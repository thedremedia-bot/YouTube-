# 1. Market research and niche decision

Researched 12 November 2026. Most public data on short-form earnings comes from
tool vendors and marketing blogs, so figures below are ranges, not facts about
what this channel will earn. Platform rules were checked against YouTube's own
help pages where possible; anything not confirmed first-hand is marked.

## 1.1 What the platforms pay, and to whom

### YouTube (the only platform that pays a South African creator per view)

- South Africa is on YouTube's list of countries where the Partner Program (YPP) is available.
- Current bar for ad revenue: **1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 watch hours on long-form videos in 12 months, or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days.** Shorts watch time does not count toward the 4,000 hours.
- There is also a lower tier (500 subscribers plus 3 million Shorts views in 90 days, or 3,000 hours) that unlocks fan funding only, not ad revenue. I did not re-verify this tier today.
- **Change on 1 February 2027.** YouTube's help page confirms updated YPP terms take effect that day but does not list numbers. Metricool, citing YouTube's blog, reports that new applicants will need 8,000 watch hours or 20 million Shorts views in 90 days, and that Shorts revenue sharing will need 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. Treat these numbers as reported, not confirmed; the real figure will show in YouTube Studio.
- Shorts pay little per view. Published ranges run from about $0.02 to $0.40 per 1,000 views, with most general-audience content near $0.03–$0.10. Viewer country matters more than niche.
- **Inauthentic content policy** (renamed July 2025, tightened July 2026). Not monetizable: content that "looks like it's made with a template", slideshows or scrolling text with little narrative, and AI content that gives "the impression of mass production". Allowed: a recurring series where each video has "a distinct storyline, focus, or concept", and a shared intro/outro. This shapes everything below.
- Realistic AI-generated or altered footage must be disclosed at upload. Clearly animated graphics do not need it. A synthetic narrator voice is a grey area; I recommend disclosing.

### TikTok

- The Creator Rewards Program covers eight countries (US, UK, Germany, Japan, South Korea, France, Mexico, Brazil). **South Africa is excluded**, as is the rest of Africa.
- Open to a South African account: LIVE gifts, brand deals (TikTok One reportedly reached South Africa in September 2026), and affiliate links in bio.
- TikTok is therefore a reach and brand-deal channel for us, not a per-view earner.

### Instagram

- No per-view Reels payout in South Africa. Bonuses are invite-only and seasonal.
- One source lists South Africa for Gifts (500 followers) and Subscriptions (10,000 followers); another source contradicts it. Check Professional Dashboard → Monetization once the account exists.
- Instagram's value is brand deals and sending traffic to a link.

**Consequence:** for a South African creator, ad revenue comes from YouTube alone,
and Shorts ad revenue is thin. The business has to be built on YouTube as the
core, with affiliate income, sponsorships, long-form videos and digital products
carrying most of the profit.

## 1.2 Niche comparison

Scores are out of 10; higher is better for us in every column (so a high
"competition" score means competition is manageable, and a high "risk" score
means low risk). The Shorts earnings column is ad revenue per million views
before costs, and is an estimate.

| Niche | Viral potential | Competition | Shorts ad earnings per 1M views (est.) | Audience size | Production cost | Ease of 2–3 a day | Sponsors and affiliates | Copyright and policy risk | Original and scalable | **Overall** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI cartoon entertainment | 8 | 4 | $20–$60 | 8 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 5 | **4.9** |
| AI animated stories | 7 | 4 | $30–$80 | 7 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 6 | **5.1** |
| Football rankings and debates | 8 | 3 | $100–$250 | 9 | 7 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 6 | **6.4** |
| Football facts and moments | 8 | 3 | $100–$250 | 9 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 2 | 4 | **5.1** |
| **Facts and educational entertainment** | 8 | 4 | $40–$150 | 10 | 9 | 9 | 7 | 9 | 8 | **7.9** |
| Reddit and mystery storytelling | 6 | 2 | $30–$100 | 7 | 9 | 9 | 4 | 4 | 3 | **4.9** |
| Animal videos and stories | 9 | 4 | $20–$60 | 9 | 3 | 3 | 5 | 3 | 3 | **4.4** |
| Visual scale and comparison explainers (emerging) | 8 | 6 | $40–$150 | 9 | 9 | 8 | 7 | 9 | 9 | **8.2** |
| History micro-stories (emerging) | 6 | 6 | $50–$150 | 6 | 6 | 6 | 6 | 7 | 7 | **6.2** |

Why each scored the way it did:

- **AI cartoons and AI animated stories.** Large audiences, but this is exactly what the July 2026 policy update targets. Reports of mass monetization suspensions for AI channels in early 2026 are unconfirmed but consistent with the policy text. Audiences skew young, which means the lowest ad rates and few sponsors. Good AI video generation costs money per clip.
- **Football rankings and debates.** Huge, passionate audience, strong in South Africa, and stats-only graphics avoid footage rights. But it is the most crowded niche on the list, opinions date quickly, and player photos and club badges are themselves rights-protected.
- **Football facts and moments.** The appeal depends on match footage. Broadcast clips draw claims and strikes even at a few seconds; three strikes ends a channel. Not a sound base for a business.
- **Reddit stories.** Cheapest to make, and for that reason saturated. Text-to-speech over gameplay footage is the textbook case of "templated" content under YouTube's policy, and the stories belong to other people.
- **Animals.** Shares very well, but you need footage. Using other people's clips is the reused-content problem; generating realistic fake animal footage is a disclosure and trust problem.
- **Facts and educational entertainment.** Largest addressable audience, all ages, global, evergreen. Advertiser-friendly. The weakness is competition from low-effort stock-footage fact channels, which is solved by having a visual style of our own.
- **Visual scale and comparison explainers.** A sub-format of facts: every video makes one surprising number visible with animation (sizes, distances, time, speed). Fewer competitors because it takes design work that most automated channels skip, every frame is ours, and each video has a distinct concept, which is what the monetization policy asks for.

## 1.3 Decision

**Niche: educational entertainment built around visual scale and comparison.**
One idea per video: a number that is bigger, smaller, older or faster than people
expect, shown with original animation.

Why it is the best business case:

1. **Lowest risk.** Every visual, word, voice track and music bed is produced by us. No footage, no characters, no logos, no borrowed stories. Facts are not copyrightable.
2. **Fits the monetization policy.** Each video has its own concept, script and custom animation.
3. **Near-zero production cost.** It can be produced entirely with the tools available to me (see [05-production-pipeline.md](05-production-pipeline.md)).
4. **Evergreen.** A video about the size of the Sun keeps earning views for years. Football opinions expire in a week.
5. **Global audience with good advertisers.** Curiosity content is watched in the US, UK and Europe, where ad rates and sponsor budgets are highest, and it is brand-safe.
6. **Leads naturally to long-form.** Themed compilations ("20 things bigger than you think") are how the channel reaches the watch-hours route into YPP and the much higher long-form ad rates.

Honest weaknesses: Shorts ad revenue is small; facts content needs strict accuracy
(one wrong number draws ridicule in the comments); and growth is slow for most
new channels. Most channels in any niche never reach 100,000 subscribers.

## 1.4 Combining formats without confusing the audience

Football is not a second niche here. It becomes one of six recurring series
inside the same promise. The channel promise is "surprising numbers made
visible"; sport appears as *The Numbers Game* (how fast a penalty really is, how
far a midfielder runs), told with the same graphics and voice as the space or
animal videos. A viewer who followed for one series still gets what they came
for from the others.

If *The Numbers Game* clearly outperforms after 30 days, the plan is to spin it
into a dedicated football-numbers channel using the same pipeline, not to tilt
this channel toward football.
